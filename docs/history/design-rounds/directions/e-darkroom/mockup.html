Preload and the Frank–Starling law

Preload is the stretch on the ventricle's muscle fibres just before it contracts. More filling stretches the fibres, and a stretched fibre contracts with more force. So, within limits, the heart pumps out what comes back to it.

Past the top of the curve, extra stretch stops helping. A failing heart sits on a flatter curve.

What sets it

Cardiac output is the volume each ventricle pumps per minute. At rest it is about 5 L/min.

  • CO = heart rate × stroke volume
  • Stroke volume = end-diastolic volume − end-systolic volume
  • Three levers on stroke volume: preload, contractility, afterload

Afterload

  • The pressure the ventricle must overcome to open the aortic valve
  • Higher afterload, less blood ejected per beat, if nothing else changes
