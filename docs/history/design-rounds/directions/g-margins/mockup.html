    Cardiac output

    NUR 112 · ch 9 lecture, 2 November

    Cardiac output is how much blood the left ventricle pumps in a minute. It is heart rate times stroke volume, about 5 L/min at rest.

    Preload and afterload

    ¶Check, from memory: why does a higher afterload lower stroke volume?
    in your own words

    ¶Yes: that is your afterload bullet, in your words. Saved to this session. ch 9 bullets

    • Preload: the stretch on the fibres at the end of diastole; tracks EDV.
    • Afterload: the pressure it must beat to open the aortic valve.
    • Contractility: squeeze at a given stretch; sympathetic tone raises it.

    More stretch, stronger beat, up to a point. In heart failure the curve flattens: extra preload buys little stroke volume and backs up into the lungs.
