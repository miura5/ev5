Ch 9 lecture · cardiac output

Cardiac output is the blood the heart pumps in a minute: CO = heart rate × stroke volume. A resting adult is about 5 L/min.

What sets stroke volume

  • Preload: the stretch at the end of filling, mostly venous return
  • Afterload: the pressure the ventricle must beat to eject
    • Rises with arterial pressure; the heart works harder
  • Contractility: the force at a given stretch
Read pp. 212–219
Draw the Frank–Starling curve
Make flashcards from this note

As the ventricle fills more, it stretches and contracts harder, so stroke volume rises. Past a point the fibres overstretch and the curve flattens; in heart failure the whole curve sits lower.

Med math rules

Weight-based: dose = kg × mg/kg. Split a daily dose by the number of doses. Liquids: (want ÷ have) × volume.
