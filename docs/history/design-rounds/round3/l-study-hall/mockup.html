Terms

Stroke volume

The amount of blood the left ventricle pushes out with each beat; about 70 mL at rest.

Cardiac output

Stroke volume × heart rate: the blood pumped per minute, about 5 L at rest.

Preload

How much the ventricle is stretched by the blood filling it at the end of diastole.

Afterload

The pressure the ventricle has to push against to open the aortic valve.

Frank–Starling law

The more the heart muscle is stretched by filling, the harder it contracts, up to a limit.

Ejection fraction

The share of blood in the ventricle that leaves with each beat; normally 55–70%.

Ch 9 lecture · cardiac output

BIO 201Oct 29Used by 2 sets

Cardiac output is how much blood the heart pumps in a minute. At rest it's about 5 L.

  • Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate
  • Stroke volume depends on preload, afterload and contractility

Preload and Frank–Starling

More filling stretches the ventricle wall. Stretched fibres overlap better, so they contract with more force. That's why stroke volume climbs as preload climbs.

Past a point the curve flattens: an over-stretched heart can't keep up, which is what happens in heart failure.

To do before Thursday

  • Redraw the curve from memory
  • Make cards for afterload and contractility
  • Ask about the ejection fraction range
